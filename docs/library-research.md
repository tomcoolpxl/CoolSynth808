# CoolSynth 808 pattern library: research notes

## How these were written

Every pattern here is my own writing of the beat onto the 808's 16-step grid. I did not copy any pattern collection, book or website, and I did not use Roland's paid pattern packs.

Very few sources publish the steps of a beat as text. Most breakdowns (Attack Magazine, MusicRadar, Muzines, City School of the Arts) show their grids only as images, and pattern sites such as drum-patterns.com keep their transcriptions behind a paywall. So I gathered what the sources say about each record: the machine, the tempo, which drums play, where the backbeat falls, the feel. Then I wrote the steps to fit both those descriptions and what the records are known to do.

That is why most beats are marked low. Low here means the beat follows the record's documented features, but the exact steps are mine and no published transcription confirms them. Medium means at least one source describes the pattern closely enough to pin down its main placements. High means two or more sources agree on the steps.

Tempos come from production articles where they exist, otherwise from DJ tempo databases (Mixmeister, Traktor and AcousticBrainz readings on cs.uwaterloo.ca and acousticbrainz.org), which are approximate. Where no figure was found, the tempo is my estimate and the notes say so.

The 808 has no ride cymbal, so a ride part goes to the closed hat. The one accent row lifts every drum on its step, so I put accents only where the backbeat or a deliberate dynamic needs them, and left ghost notes unaccented.

## Electro and early hip hop

### Electro 1982

After Afrika Bambaataa & the Soulsonic Force, "Planet Rock". It was the first hit built on the 808 and the root of electro, Miami bass and Detroit techno.

Sound on Sound's Classic Tracks piece has Arthur Baker explaining that the session player was asked to copy the beat of Kraftwerk's "Numbers", and that they dropped the snare to keep only kick and handclaps. MusicRadar's electro tutorial adds the open hat on the last sixteenth.

I wrote kick on steps 1, 7 and 11 with a pickup in bar two, claps on 2 and 4, and sixteenth hats. Tempo estimates run from 122 to 128 depending on the release; I used 127.

Doubt: drum-patterns.com's summary also mentions a cowbell, which I could not confirm, so it is left out.

### Electro 1983

After Cybotron, "Clear", often called the first Detroit techno record as well as an electro cornerstone.

Attack Magazine's breakdown gives 125 BPM, straight timing and snare plus clap on 2 and 4. Its kick and hat grid is an image I could not read, so those steps are my reconstruction.

### West Coast electro 1984

After Egyptian Lover, "Egypt, Egypt", the Los Angeles anthem of a producer known as the king of the 808.

Red Bull Music Academy, Splice and Roland describe how he learned the accent and tempo controls and arranged the record like a DJ megamix, but none gives the steps. The tempo is a DJ-database figure. This is a reconstruction in his style, with an accented tom run closing bar two.

### Hip hop 1984

After T La Rock & Jazzy Jay, "It's Yours", Rick Rubin's first Def Jam beat.

Native Instruments and Roland both say Rubin turned the kick's decay all the way up so the kick works as a bassline, over snare and claps. Passion of the Weiss covers the record's making. The steps and tempo are mine.

### Hip hop 1986

After the Beastie Boys, "Paul Revere". Wikipedia, Songfacts and uDiscover agree that Adam Yauch made the beat on an 808 and played it backwards from tape.

The pattern is the rhythm as heard, on long-decay kick and snare. To get the record's swelling sound, render the loop and reverse it.

Doubt: the steps are a reconstruction, and the reversal makes hit positions hard to judge by ear.

## R&B, soul and pop

### Electronic soul 1982

After Marvin Gaye, "Sexual Healing", the first 808 hit in mainstream R&B, programmed by Gaye himself.

Attack Magazine gives the clearest description of any beat here: 94 BPM, a light 52% swing, a loose almost reggae kick over two bars, a snare used like a hat, claves, only two high-tom notes in two bars, and claps once a bar instead of on 2 and 4. Gearnews confirms the hats, claves, claps and toms. Native Instruments mentions subtle snare fills and use of the accent.

I placed the single clap on beat 3, the one-drop of the reggae feel Attack describes. That placement is my reading and the main doubt.

### Minneapolis R&B 1983

After the S.O.S. Band, "Just Be Good to Me". Gearnews says the drum pattern is one of the song's main features, and Wikipedia confirms the 808.

The tempo, about 101, comes from AcousticBrainz. The steps are a reconstruction.

### Freestyle 1983

After Shannon, "Let the Music Play", the record that named freestyle.

Wikipedia and Roland confirm the 808 and the hard-gated reverb on kick and snare. Roland says Chris Barbosa slowed the tempo. Red Bull Music Academy says the drum sound came from Arthur Baker's "Looking for the Perfect Beat".

The 3-3-2 kick and the tom fill are my reading. The tempo is an estimate.

### Synth-pop 1983

After New Order, "Blue Monday". It was programmed on an Oberheim DMX, not an 808, and is included at the user's request.

Reverb, MusicRadar and Muzines' "The Rhythm Method" all describe the famous sixteenth-note kick, which MusicRadar traces to Giorgio Moroder's delayed kick on Donna Summer's "Our Love". Muzines adds that Stephen Morris found a triplet-like echo through the DMX's kick dynamics and then left the dynamics out of the final pattern. That is why no accent is used here.

I wrote four-on-the-floor with an eight-sixteenth run on beats 3 and 4 of every second bar, as the intro is usually remembered. Muzines' grid is an image I could not read, so the exact length of the run is my reading.

### Dance-pop 1987

After Whitney Houston, "I Wanna Dance with Somebody (Who Loves Me)". In Mix, Narada Michael Walden says he and Preston Glass programmed his 808. Roland names the handclaps, cowbells and booming kicks.

The cowbell's figure is my reading of the record.

### New jack swing 1988

A style tribute after Teddy Riley: Keith Sweat's "I Want Her" and Bobby Brown's "My Prerogative".

Wikipedia, Mixdown and the Red Bull Music Academy oral history describe the swung 808 and SP-1200 sound and the triplet fills. The heavy 66% swing and the triplet roll into bar three follow those descriptions. No source gives a tempo; 106 is my estimate.

### Electro-pop R&B 2008

After Kanye West, "Love Lockdown", from the album that put "808" in its title.

Roland and Wikipedia describe the heartbeat 808 kick and the taiko drums in the chorus. Wikipedia gives about 120 BPM.

Variation B gives the taiko part to the 808 toms. The kick's double beat is my reading.

### Stomp pop 2005

After Gwen Stefani, "Hollaback Girl" (the Neptunes), added at the user's request.

Wikipedia covers the production but not the drums. DJ databases and a karaoke site give 110 to 111. The stomp-and-clap and the marching-snare bar are my reading of the record.

Doubt: the search found nothing linking the beat to Queen's "We Will Rock You", so the notes claim no such link.

### West Coast minimal 2004

After Snoop Dogg featuring Pharrell, "Drop It Like It's Hot", added at the user's request.

Wikipedia lists tongue clicks, keyboards, a drum machine beat and white noise. Flavorwire adds a tabla sample. A DJ database gives 92 to 93 BPM.

The clicks go to the rim shot and the white noise to the snare. The click figure is my reading.

## Miami bass and the South

### Miami bass 1985

After MC A.D.E., "Bass Rock Express", widely called the start of Miami bass.

Complex credits it as the first record with the sustained 808 kick. Red Bull Music Academy's interview has Amos Larkins claiming the production.

The steps and tempo are a reconstruction.

### Miami bass 1986

After 2 Live Crew, "Throw the D".

The Red Bull Music Academy interview and Wikipedia say Mr. Mixx built it on an 808 with sustained kicks, plus samples of "Dance to the Drummer's Beat" and "Planet Rock". The tempo, 123, is a DJ-database reading.

The steps are a reconstruction, and the sampled break is left out.

### Atlanta bass 1993

After Tag Team, "Whoomp! (There It Is)". The tempo, 129, is from a DJ database.

Doubt: the sources do not reliably confirm an 808 on this record. The pattern is a reconstruction of the party-bass groove.

### Jacksonville bass 1994

After 69 Boyz, "Tootsee Roll", produced by Quad City DJ's. DJ databases give 131 to 133.

The drum programming is undocumented; this is a reconstruction.

### Bounce 1986

After the Showboys, "Drag Rap", whose 808 section became the Triggerman beat that New Orleans bounce is built on.

Red Bull Music Academy's Loop History quotes the producers: a DMX hard kick for the intro, and the 808 for the rest, laid down as bass drum, snare, cowbell, then hi-hat. Afropop describes the kicks-and-cowbells section as a mid-tempo strut.

The syncopation and the tempo are my reading.

### Memphis rap 1995

A style tribute after DJ Spanish Fly and early Three 6 Mafia.

Future Audio Workshop and Splice name the 808 sub, cowbell, syncopation and sharp snares. Wikipedia covers the scene. The slow tempo and hat rolls are my reading.

Doubt: a forum post claims the SP-1200 for DJ Paul and Juicy J. Unverified.

### New Orleans hip hop 1999

After Mannie Fresh's work for Cash Money, above all Juvenile's "Back That Azz Up".

Roland says nearly the whole rhythm track is built from 808 sounds. Wikipedia calls it a distinctive bounce beat. The steps and tempo are a reconstruction.

Doubt: Roland's article gives both 1998 and 1999 for the record.

### Crunk 2003

After Lil Jon & the East Side Boyz with Ying Yang Twins, "Get Low".

Apple Music's description of his "boom-boom-clap" 808 beats gives the core figure. A DJ database gives 101 BPM. Roland stresses the low end. Medium, because the figure is clear from the description.

### Crunk&B 2004

After Usher, "Yeah!".

The tempo, 105, is from the published sheet music. Wikipedia and Red Bull Music Academy cover the crunk&B production.

Doubt: no source confirms 808 sounds on this record, so it is a crunk-style tribute.

### Snap 2005

After D4L's "Laffy Taffy" and Dem Franchize Boyz' "Lean wit It, Rock wit It".

Wikipedia's snap entry lists an 808 kick, hi-hat, bass and finger snaps in place of the snare. Stereogum covers the record. The 808 has no snap, so the claves stand in for it. The tempo is my estimate.

### Snap 2007

After Soulja Boy, "Crank That (Soulja Boy)", made entirely from FL Studio's stock sounds (Wikipedia, Vice). It was not made on an 808.

A Beatport listing gives 70 BPM. The kit pattern is my reading.

## Trap and drill

### Trap 2010

A style tribute after Lex Luger, above all Waka Flocka Flame's "Hard in da Paint".

Native Instruments describes Luger's note-repeat hat triplets and bouncy 808. Its trap guide describes the half-time snare. No reliable tempo was found; 150 is my estimate.

### Chicago drill 2012

A style tribute after Young Chop's beats for Chief Keef, above all "I Don't Like".

XXL and Complex cover the beat's history but not its drums. A database entry for the remix gives 131. The pattern is my reading.

### Atlanta trap 2016

After Metro Boomin, above all Migos' "Bad and Boujee". A Medium analysis gives 127 BPM and the hook's barrage of triplets.

The grid has no true triplets, so "3" rolls stand in for them. That approximation is the main doubt.

### UK drill 2018

After the London drill sound.

Attack Magazine's breakdown describes the hat figure on a sixteenth grid ("two spaces, two spaces and one space") and ghost snares. Beatstorapon puts the snare on beat 3 of bar one and beat 4 of bar two. Other guides give 138 to 145 BPM.

Medium, because those placements are spelled out in text. The sliding 808 bass is melodic and outside this kit.

### Brooklyn drill 2019

After 808Melo's beats for Pop Smoke.

Roland's guide describes the bass slides and the space left in the beat. Wikipedia covers 808Melo. This is UK drill's skeleton with more air; the tempo is my estimate.

## Acid house and Chicago house

### Proto-acid 1982

After Charanjit Singh, "Synthesizing: Ten Ragas to a Disco Beat". It used an 808, a 303 and a Jupiter-8 five years before Chicago acid (Wikipedia, Roland, DJ Mag).

No source describes the drums. This is the plain disco beat the title promises, at an estimated tempo.

### Chicago house 1984

After Jesse Saunders and Vince Lawrence, "On and On", recorded on an 808 and widely called the first house record (Wikipedia, MusicRadar, Medium).

MusicRadar names the four-on-the-floor kick and the shuffling snares, which gives this medium. The snare steps and the tempo are mine.

### Deep house 1985

After Mr. Fingers, "Mystery of Love".

Larry Heard's own account (WNYC, Red Bull Music Academy) names a TR-707, not an 808; Wikipedia's Larry Heard article says 909. It is played here on 808 sounds as the deep house skeleton.

### Piano house 1986

After Marshall Jefferson, "Move Your Body".

Wikipedia lists his gear purchase but not the drum machine's model, and Jefferson appears on lists of 808 users. A reconstruction.

### Acid house 1987

After Phuture, "Acid Tracks", the founding acid record and the one the user cares about most.

Roland and Wikipedia say the drums were a TR-707 with a TR-727. Roland gives 119 BPM, Wikipedia 120. DJ Mag covers the record's history.

The 727's Latin percussion goes to the 808 congas. Medium for the kit and tempo; the conga steps are mine.

### UK acid house 1988

After A Guy Called Gerald, "Voodoo Ray".

Sound on Sound, Red Bull Music Academy and MusicRadar confirm the 808 at the centre of his setup. Native Instruments describes a syncopated beat, subtle snare fills and hats given life by the accent. One catalogue gives 120 BPM. The steps are my reconstruction.

### Chicago acid house 1988

After Bam Bam, "Where's Your Child?". 909 Originals and Fact describe it as slower and more brooding than other early acid.

The drum machine is unconfirmed. A bare reconstruction.

### Chicago house 1989

After Lil Louis, "French Kiss". DJ Mag's Point Blank deconstruction names 808 and 707 drums. Wikipedia describes the slowdown to a stop and back.

A minimal reconstruction. Ride the tempo by hand to get the record's famous move.

### UK house 1989

After 808 State, "Pacific State". Despite the band's name, Graham Massey says the drums were a 909 and that the clap pattern matters most (Equipboard, Sound on Sound).

The clap figure is my reading.

### Ghetto house 1995

After the Dance Mania sound of DJ Funk.

Attack Magazine's ghetto house breakdown spells out the parts in text: 130 to 135 BPM, 50 to 55% swing, four-on-the-floor with a quieter ghost kick on the last off-beat, claps on 2 and 4, off-beat hats and one open hat in bar two. Medium, from one clear source. Its own sounds are 909, 505 and 606.

### Footwork 2010

A style tribute after RP Boo and DJ Rashad.

Wikipedia, Roland and MusicRadar give 150 to 165 BPM, syncopated skipping kicks and 808 toms. Groupings of three stand in for the genre's triplet feel.

## Detroit

### Detroit techno 1985

After Model 500, "No UFO's".

Attack Magazine's deconstruction gives 125 BPM: a 909 kick in 4/4, an 808 cowbell, 808 closed and open hats, a snare-clap combo, and a second, probably 808, kick playing sixteenths. Sound on Sound says the beats came from a 909 chained to a DrumTraks.

Both kicks share one lane here, with the main pulse accented. Medium.

### Minimal techno 1993

After Plastikman, "Spastik". Wikipedia says it is built on 808 percussion, and Roland's guide calls it techno made from the sounds of a single drum machine.

The kick plus a web of snare rolls is a reconstruction. On the record Hawtin also shifts the snares' panning and pitch live.

### Ghettotech 1997

A style tribute after DJ Assault and Detroit booty music.

The Red Bull Music Academy oral history and Wikipedia give 145 to 160 BPM, clipped 808s and fast edits.

### Detroit electro 1999

A style tribute after Drexciya's Neptune's Lair era. Red Bull says the 808 was central to James Stinson's work, and LANDR describes Drexciya's gritty, minimal 808 sound.

Doubt: Attack's "Black Sea" breakdown shows they also played a 909, and Roland ties "Rublick's Cube" to a 606.

## Breakbeats and big beat

### Funk break 1969

The Amen break, from the Winstons' "Amen, Brother", the most sampled four bars in music and the base of jungle.

Ethan Hein's breakdown and listening guide, MusicRadar's lesson and Wikipedia agree on the outline. Bars one and two are a funk pattern with a doubled kick on the and of 3 and snare pickups. Bar three delays the second backbeat. Bar four drops the first kick, doubles the kick on the and of 1, adds a snare on the second sixteenth and an early crash on step 11. Wikipedia gives about 136 BPM, MusicRadar 137.

High. The four bars are written as two chained patterns.

### Funk break 1970

Clyde Stubblefield's break on James Brown's "Funky Drummer".

Ethan Hein's step-by-step build, Roland's Behind the Beat and MusicRadar's MIDI tutorial agree. Kick on 1, the and of 1, the and of 2 and the and of 3, plus a sixteenth before 4. Loud backbeats with ghost snares around them. Open hats before beat 3 and after beat 4.

High for the kick, snare and open hats. The ghost-note velocities rest on the accent row, and swing stands in for Stubblefield's push.

### Big beat 1997

After the Chemical Brothers, "Block Rockin' Beats". Ali Jamieson identifies the break as Bernard Purdie's "Changes", a roomy ride break with a bigger kick layered on and heavy compression. DJ databases give 109.4 BPM.

The steps are my reading. Most of the style happens in the compression after the machine.

### Breakbeat rave 1996

A style tribute after the Prodigy's "Firestarter" and "Breathe". Beatport lists "Firestarter" at 142. A KVR thread notes that the Prodigy break up their beats with no steady hat.

I found no source for the break's origin.

### Big beat 1998

A style tribute after Fatboy Slim, "The Rockafeller Skank". Wikipedia lists the samples, including the drum intro of "I Fought the Law", and notes the track's tempo changes. A Beatport edit gives 153.

The steps are mine.

## IDM

### Ambient acid 1991

After Aphex Twin's Analogue Bubblebath EP, his acid side. MusicRadar's acid house overview names the 1991 AFX release among 808-led acid records. FACT ties the later Analord series to the 909, 303 and SH-101, so I followed Analogue Bubblebath rather than Analord.

Doubt: the 808 on this EP rests on MusicRadar alone. The steps and tempo are a tribute.

### Drill 'n' bass 1996

After Squarepusher, "Feed Me Weird Things". Bandcamp Daily and Wikipedia describe the high-speed drum fills and jungle breaks.

Four bars over two chained patterns, ending in a long roll. A tribute; the tempo is my estimate.

## Jungle

### Darkcore jungle 1992

A style tribute after Rufige Kru (Goldie), "Terminator", known for its time-stretched breaks.

Wikipedia's jungle article and MusicRadar's jungle tutorials give the 160 BPM frame and the slice-and-resequence method. Rolls stand in for the stretched stutters.

Doubt: I found no source for this record's own drums, and there is no Wikipedia page for the track.

### Ragga jungle 1994

After Shy FX & UK Apache, "Original Nuttah". K Mag says the Amen is used in an unusual shuffling pattern that feels frenzied. Wikipedia covers the record.

The re-cut second bar and the 170 tempo are my reading.

### Jungle 1994

A style tribute after the chopped-Amen edits of DJ Hype and Remarc. Loopmasters and MusicRadar describe slicing, re-sequencing and snare rolls; Mixmag's Amen list covers the records.

Four bars: two plain, two edited.

### Atmospheric jungle 1995

After LTJ Bukem, "Horizons". The Vinyl Factory and A Bass Chronicle describe the lush, rolling Good Looking sound.

MusicRadar's two-step description sets the kick an eighth before the second snare. The tempo and the ghost notes are mine.

Doubt: I could not confirm whether the record uses the Think break.

## Left out, and why

Yellow Magic Orchestra's "1000 Knives", the first 808 record (1980 live, 1981 on BGM). I found nothing about its pattern and did not want to invent one.

Hashim's "Al-Naafiysh" and Loose Ends' "Hangin' on a String". Roland confirms the 808 on both, but nothing describes their rhythm, and the electro and R&B lanes were already covered.

Drift phonk. Its defining part is a cowbell melody played at many pitches, and the 808's single-pitch cowbell cannot play it.

Brazilian funk's "Volt Mix" and DJ Magic Mike's "Drop the Bass". Both are named in Roland's guide, but I found no rhythm descriptions; worth a later pass.
